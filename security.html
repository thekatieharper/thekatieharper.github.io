Personal Security Checklist

I keep a living checklist for people in and around defense tech: founders, investors, contractors, and anyone who lives near a base. It is the ordinary stuff: the steps that make stolen logins, new credit in your name, and an easy-to-find home address harder. I add to it as those basics change.

Accounts

  • Use one password manager. One vault is easier to audit than passwords scattered across notes and memory.
  • Delete passwords saved in the browser. A browser is a convenient place to sign in and a poor place to keep the keys to everything else.
  • Turn on passkeys or 2FA everywhere, email first. Email is how almost every other account gets recovered.
  • Retire or lock down old recovery emails. An inbox you no longer watch is still a way back in.
  • Review third-party app access. Old connections keep working after you stop using the app.
  • Rotate anything that was stored in a breached password manager. If that vault was exposed, those passwords are no longer private.

Money and Identity

  • Freeze your credit at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. A freeze blocks most new credit opened in your name, and you place it separately at each bureau.
  • Optionally freeze ChexSystems, NCTUE, and Innovis too. Banks, phone and utility companies, and some lenders check files the big three do not.
  • Get an IRS Identity Protection PIN. It stops someone else from filing a tax return with your Social Security number, and you will need it when you file.
  • Set a carrier port-out PIN and a SIM lock. The port-out PIN blocks a stranger from moving your number to a phone they control, and a SIM lock stops a loose SIM from being used in another device.

Public Records

  • Forming an LLC can put your home address in the state registry, so use a commercial registered agent and a business address. That filing is public, and a home address on it is easy to look up.
  • Learn your county's address-exemption rules for property records. Deeds and tax rolls are public, and some places let eligible people keep a home address off them.
  • Use Google's Results about you. It is where you ask Google to remove search results that show personal information.

Data Brokers

  • Use a removal service. It covers the large brokers that republish names, phone numbers, and addresses.
  • Do the manual opt-outs it misses, including business-record sites and relatives' profiles. A service will not catch every listing, and a family member's page can still point back to you.

Home and Devices

  • Turn off smart-TV automatic content recognition (ACR) and ad tracking. ACR watches what is on the screen and sends it out for ads.
  • Secure the router. Change the default admin password, turn on updates, and use a strong Wi-Fi password.
  • Put IoT devices on a guest network. A camera, speaker, or bulb should not sit on the same network as your laptop.
  • Tighten phone privacy settings. Location access, app permissions, and ad tracking stay on until you turn them off.
  • Turn off car data sharing. Connected cars upload location and driving data until you turn that sharing off.

OPSEC Habits

  • Keep base and home locations off public profiles. A base, unit, or neighborhood makes it easier to find where you live and work.
  • Post travel after you are back. A real-time post says the house is empty.
  • Use email aliases and a virtual mailbox for signups. An alias keeps your real inbox off forms, and a virtual mailbox keeps your home address off them.

Run through it once. Run through it again when you move, change jobs, or start a company.
